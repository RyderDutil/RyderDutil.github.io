images/dice/main.png

Balanced Six-Sided Die Assembly

Final project for ME 350: Computer-Aided Design and Manufacturing at UNR. Designed a balanced six-sided die achieving micrometer-level center-of-mass accuracy across six distinct materials (PLA, brass, steel, acrylic, wood, and aluminum) with varying densities and discrete thickness constraints. A custom MATLAB solver using multi-start optimization swept discrete thickness combinations to simultaneously satisfy moment of inertia, center-of-mass, and products of inertia tolerances. All three grading error metrics were met: CG error 0.115% (req. <2%), moment error 1.495% (req. <3%), and product error 0.122% (req. <0.2%). Deliverables included individual part drawings for each face, an exploded assembly view, a full BOM, and verified mass properties from SolidWorks.

Electromechanical Rail Gun Analysis

Systems and controls analysis of a linear DC machine modeled as a prototype rail gun, coupling electrical and mechanical subsystems via a shared magnetic field. Derived a 2×2 MIMO transfer function matrix using Laplace transforms and Cramer's rule, analyzed pole locations and dynamic stability, and evaluated steady-state performance limits including maximum safe sled speed and stall conditions under a 5 kA current constraint. Responsible for MATLAB implementation of Bode plots, inductance sensitivity analysis, and full Simulink verification of all transfer function blocks.
